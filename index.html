The core value

AI Insights

Not statistics — interpretation. What the signals mean, who benefits, and what could shift.

INSIGHT 01

Satellite connectivity is becoming critical infrastructure

Commercial LEO connectivity is moving from a consumer novelty to a foundational layer of national and enterprise resilience.
Why it matters
Resilience budgets, not consumer ARPU, are becoming the demand driver — a broader, stickier market.
Potential winners
LEO operators with enterprise and government channels · ground-segment suppliers
Potential risks
Regulatory hesitation · single-operator dependency in critical systems
INSIGHT 02

Launch capacity is concentrating among a few providers

A small number of dominant launch operators now handle most of the mass deployed — a structural feature, not a temporary one.
Why it matters
Pricing power and deployment timelines for the whole sector depend on a handful of players.
Potential winners
Vertically integrated operators · reusable-launch challengers
Potential risks
Bottleneck risk · cascading delays from a single provider
INSIGHT 03

Demand for resilient connectivity is rising fast

Maritime, remote, and enterprise sectors are turning backup satellite links into primary connectivity.
Why it matters
A high-margin, contracted revenue layer that the consumer market cannot replicate.
Potential winners
Managed-service integrators · maritime and energy verticals
Potential risks
Latency-sensitive use cases · capacity contention over hot spots
INSIGHT 04

The opportunity is shifting to the ground segment

The next pool of value is in ground infrastructure and network orchestration — the layer that makes orbital capacity usable.
Why it matters
Less capital-intensive than satellites, with software-like margins and stronger defensibility.
Potential winners
Orchestration software · gateway and terminal manufacturers
Potential risks
Standards wars · operator vertical integration crowding out third parties

Alcyone Space Economy Index — Methodology v1.0

What it measures

The Alcyone Space Economy Index is a synthetic 0–100 reading of the state of the global space economy at a given point in time. It aggregates four market dimensions into a single composite figure.

The four components
ComponentWeightWhat it reflects
Connectivity30%Market adoption and connectivity signals
Infrastructure30%Constellation deployment status and infrastructure signals
Launch Activity20%Launch provider status and positive market signals
Market Momentum20%New entrants and competitive intensity

Global Index = 0.30 × Connectivity + 0.30 × Infrastructure + 0.20 × Launch + 0.20 × Market Momentum.

Score vs momentum

Each component carries two distinct readings:

Score — the current structural state (how strong the dimension is).

Momentum — the rate of change over time (how fast it is moving), computed only from real historical observations. Momentum is never folded into the Score.

Data sources

Component inputs draw on public data — Space-Track (active satellite counts per constellation), Launch Library 2 / The Space Devs (launch cadence) — combined with structured expert assessment of operator and market status.

Confidence

Each dynamic metric carries a confidence level (High / Medium / Low) reflecting data coverage — how many operators or sources contribute to the reading.

Honesty rules

Historical values are never backfilled or retro-calculated.

Momentum requires at least two real observations; otherwise it is reported as Baseline — never zero, never estimated, never invented.

No data → no score. No history → no momentum claim. No source → no metric.

Versioning

Methodology v1.0 · Initial production methodology · launched September 2026. Weights and sources are published; substantive changes will be versioned (v1.1, v2.0…).
